Language models solve most long-arithmetic problems, but this accuracy is estimated on samples and guarantees nothing for unseen inputs. We show that exactness at all input lengths becomes a finite, checkable property when each computational step reads only a bounded view of an external working memory. In Sketchpad, a 0.8-billion-parameter vision-language model with a low-rank adapter executes arithmetic on a rendered sheet, reading three cells and emitting one step per forward pass. We prove in Lean that the model is exact on all inputs if and only if it is exact on 2,446 views, and that no finite test suffices when steps read earlier steps. All views pass on a laptop GPU in a browser. The certified model answered 220 of 220 long sums and products, against 217 for the best of ten other models, and supports exact polynomial algebra; a gate on the adapter recovers the base model exactly.
