# Venue judgement: Sketchpad

Reviewer: Fable (Claude), 31 October 2026. Read: `paper.md`, `arxiv/PRIOR_ART_2026-10-31.md`, `review/CONTROLS.md`, the live demo, and Cai 2017, Lewandowski 2026, CAEF 2024 and Gross 2024. Calibrated against what Nature Machine Intelligence (NMI) and Nature Communications (NC) published on these topics in 2024–2026 (sources at the end). Odds are estimates.

## 1. Verdicts

**What an editor sees at first pass.** A 0.8B vision-language model with a LoRA adapter runs pencil-and-paper arithmetic one column per forward pass; because a step sees only three cells, checking 2,446 views certifies every length; the check runs on the laptop GPU in a browser; 220/220 where GPT-6.1 Sol scores 217. The editor's first three thoughts, in order:

1. The principle (bounded view, hence finite check, hence all lengths) is classical, and the paper says so (Moore 1956, Cai 2017, Lewandowski 2026).
2. The network does the job of a 2,446-row lookup table at 0.1 s per step: by the paper's cost model a 1,000-digit sum takes about 100 s, a calculator microseconds. Why neural?
3. The glyphs are fixed sprites, so the "vision" is lookup of exact images; no perception is certified either.

The editor will also see Lean proofs, pre-registration, a device-path certificate, open weights and a demo, a necessity theorem aimed at chain of thought, and a frontier comparison. The paper is complete and honest; its problem is significance, not quality.

**Nature Machine Intelligence (Article).** NMI's 2024–26 output on these topics is thin and tilted to broad claims: ChemCrow (tool use in chemistry, 2024), the Ito et al. Perspective on algorithmic generalisation as a measure of AI (2025), "Densing law of LLMs" (2025). I found no NMI Article in 2024–26 on neural-network verification or on arithmetic length generalisation; that literature lives at NeurIPS/ICLR/CAV. Sketchpad would read as a well-executed demonstration of a known principle in a toy domain. Likely first-pass conclusion: "of interest to specialists; a specialist venue is more suitable." Sent to review ~25%; accepted if reviewed ~30% (a verification reviewer says "Cai 2017 plus brute force", an LLM reviewer says "use a calculator; GSM8K did not improve"). Overall ~7–8%.

**Nature Communications.** NC publishes complete system papers at this scale (MiniCPM-V, an 8B on-device multimodal model, NC 2025) and accepts specialist advances when reproducible and well controlled. Sent to review ~50%; accepted after review ~40%, probably after a round asking for more models or a downstream task. Overall ~20%. Main risks: "incremental over CAEF and Cai", and "the certified module brought no benefit".

Neither is a good bet as it stands. ICLR/NeurIPS main track or TMLR would take the current paper with high probability.

## 2. Closest prior art

**Cai, Shin, Song 2017 (ICLR).** A Neural Programmer-Interpreter with recursion on a scratch pad; because recursion bounds what each subprogram sees, they enumerate every input a subprogram can meet and prove perfect generalisation for grade-school addition and three sorting algorithms. This *is* Theorem 1's argument, nine years earlier. Sketchpad adds the carrier (a pretrained VLM), the operations (×, ÷, √, gcd), floating-point margins with a device certificate, Lean, and the necessity theorem. A reviewer who knows Cai will say the concept is Cai's and the rest is engineering: not enough for NMI alone; adequate for NC if the engineering is framed as the contribution.

**Lewandowski, Machado, Schuurmans 2026 (and Schuurmans 2024).** They prove that chaining outputs is universal and verify Llama-4-17B under a found system prompt on each of the 1,857 rules of a universal Lag system (a string-rewriting machine that reads the first symbols of a string and appends). Their loop keeps the growing string outside the model and feeds only the prompt plus the encoded symbol pair, so their policy is pinned exactly as Sketchpad's is, and the extension to all inputs is a theorem by induction, not an assumption. (An earlier draft of this report claimed otherwise; withdrawn, see the Discussion.) The honest distinction is in the certificate and the carrier, not the logic: they check exact output match at temperature zero on a server, with no floating-point account, on a prompted 17B model, and their Gemini-1.5 result can no longer be re-verified because the model was deprecated; Sketchpad measures logit margins in the deployment numerics with a robustness theorem, binds the certificate to open weights by hash, and recomputes it on the user's device, with a trained 0.8B model. Their claim is universal computation; ours is arithmetic. An editor will read Sketchpad as the same certificate structure on a narrower claim, done more carefully.

**CAEF, Lai et al. 2024.** Llama-3.1-8B fine-tuned to execute seven operators step by step as a Turing machine on a text tape; near-100% at 100 digits; one adapter per operator; no guarantee, and the context retains earlier steps, so Theorem 2 applies to it. Sketchpad has a guarantee, one adapter, a 10× smaller model, √ and gcd, and runs on-device. An editor reads this as "CAEF with a certificate", so the certificate must carry the paper, and it is the classical argument.

**Gross et al. 2024 (NeurIPS).** Computer-checked proofs of a trained one-layer transformer's accuracy on Max-of-K: brute force over all inputs, and compact proofs derived from the mechanism. In their hierarchy Sketchpad's certificate is the weakest form, brute force over a small domain; what lifts it to all lengths is the runtime, which is conventional code, and the mechanism section proves nothing from the circuit it describes. Those who know this work will call 2,446 enumerated inputs exhaustive testing, not verification.

**Not in the prior-art file; check:** Discrete Neural Algorithmic Reasoning (Rodionov and Prokhorenkova, 2024), which forces finite discrete execution states so execution can be checked exactly. Ito et al. 2026 (provably correct tiny transformer) shares an author with the NMI 2025 Perspective: a likely reviewer.

## 3. Ranked upgrades

**1. Show the certified executor helps the language model (application value).** Abstract: "With the sheet, the 4B dialogue model solves X% of word problems with 15–40-digit quantities, against Y% for the base model and Z% for GPT-6.1 Sol, every number certified." Why editors care: it converts a method into a capability and answers "why not a calculator" with "the calculator is the model, offline, with the guarantee on the network." Feasibility: the 4B model is training; build a bottleneck benchmark (GSM8K-style problems with large numbers, finance, unit conversion); 1–2 weeks, under $300 on Modal. Kill: the 7% translation errors swamp the gain, or a Python-tool baseline matches and the only edge left is "no interpreter needed".

**2. Bridge Theorem 2 to frontier failures (theory with reach).** Theorem 2's context-dependent logit drift is unmeasured on real models. Measure it: on open models (Qwen3.5-27B, Gemma 4) doing long sums with chain of thought, record the per-digit margin against position and length, and show the silent errors at 300–1,000 digits appear where drift exceeds margin, while the pinned sheet holds margins constant. Abstract: "In chain-of-thought arithmetic the step margin falls with context length, so no finite test certifies these models." Why editors care: a mechanism for silent frontier failure with a constructive alternative is an insight, the genre NMI prints. Feasibility: 1–2 weeks, under $200. Kill: the errors turn out to be digit misalignment (AI-rithmetic 2026 attributes 63–92% to misalignment or carry), and the result weakens to "consistent with".

**3. Generality across models and a second domain.** Certify the same tables on two or three other bases (Gemma 4 small, SmolVLM, Qwen3.5-2B) and add the Lean rewrite-chain asset as a second certified domain. Abstract: "The recipe certifies three model families and term rewriting." Why: shows the method, not the model, is the contribution. 2–3 weeks, ~$300. Kill: a base fails to reach margins; report it as a limit.

**4. A certified general interpreter (scope).** A small instruction set on the sheet (registers, compare, branch) with a finite view set. Abstract: "A certified neural executor runs any program of an N-instruction set exactly." Why: removes "arithmetic is a toy". 4–8 weeks. Kill: the why-neural objection returns stronger ("a CPU built from a lookup table"); the latent-slot executor cannot be certified without a finite quotient of its state, which is months.

**5. Perception robustness.** Certify over N fonts × offsets (still finite). One week. Marginal; only as an answer to objection 3.

**The single change that most moves the decision** is upgrade 2 made the headline. It changes the paper's genre from demonstration ("we certified a small model") to explanation ("here is why sampled accuracy cannot certify chain of thought, measured on real models, and here is the discipline under which it can"). The arithmetic becomes the worked example rather than the claim, which blunts objections 1–3 at first pass. Upgrade 1 is the necessary companion for NC, whose reviewers ask "does it help?" first.

## 4. Recommended path

1. **Post to arXiv now,** with the Lewandowski contrast stated honestly (margin certificate in the deployment numerics on a trained small open model, against exact-match checks at temperature zero on a prompted 17B) and DNAR cited. Priority is established; nothing below is blocked.
2. **Three to four weeks, in parallel:** upgrade 2 and upgrade 1 (with a Python-tool baseline), under $600 together; upgrade 3 if a week is spare.
3. **Decision gate.** If upgrade 2 is clean on at least two open models, retitle around finite certification of computation in language models and submit to NMI, with the arithmetic as the demonstration and upgrade 1 in support. Otherwise submit to NC with upgrades 1 and 3 under the present title. Either way, raise the lookup-table objection yourself and answer it (the same weights are the chat model; the guarantee is on the trained network, not on a wrapper).
4. **Fallback.** On a desk rejection, ICLR 2027 or TMLR with the same manuscript; near-certain. Do not start upgrade 4 before a journal decision.

## Discussion with Opus

Replies to `VENUE_MINE.md`, 31 October 2026. We agree on the verdicts; the points below are where I move.

**1. Feasibility of my upgrade 2.** You are right, and I withdraw it as a headline. In the comparison the open models fail by exhausting the budget (Qwen3.5-27B 93/99, DeepSeek 17/21); only Gemma 4 31B produces completed wrong answers (46/64), and the models whose errors are silent expose no logits. A drift-to-error link resting on one open model is a figure, not a claim. On plausibility: a reasoning model re-derives each digit, so a monotone fall of the step margin with context length is not what I would expect; the likelier mechanism is misalignment of digit positions (AI-rithmetic 2026), which is a context effect but not the drift Theorem 2 constructs. Salvage: a forced-direct-answer run on Gemma 4 31B and Qwen3.5-27B, per-digit margin by position, as a supporting panel for Theorem 2. One week, under $100, no headline weight.

**2. Your upgrade 1 and DNAR.** DNAR (Rodionov and Prokhorenkova, ICML 2025) pre-empts it. Its abstract: the architecture "allows us to prove the correctness of the learned algorithms for any test data", by forcing execution through finite predefined states and checking the processor's step on every state transition. That is Theorem 1 applied to CLRS algorithms (sorting, BFS, shortest paths), trained from scratch, already published. A certified CLRS subset on a pretrained model would add exactly the increment the paper already has over Cai 2017 (pretrained carrier, device certificate), so it duplicates the novelty rather than adding a kind. It also sets up a head-to-head with DNAR that we would not win on breadth. DNAR is further a prior-art risk for the present paper: it must be cited with the distinction stated (pretrained model; floating-point certificate on the deployment path; the necessity theorem; composition). On your question: cross-model certification (my upgrade 3) is the cheaper "method, not model" move and does not re-tread DNAR, so I rank it above a CLRS subset, but neither moves an NMI editor on its own.

**3. The Lewandowski claim.** Withdrawn; see the correction below. Do not put it in the arXiv version.

**4. The lookup-table objection.** Your candidate is honest and is the right frame; "any network that memorises 2,446 rows passes" is not a weakness of the certificate, since a certificate should not care how the function is computed. The strongest honest answer adds two things a table cannot do. First, the table cannot be the chat model: the same weights decide when to compute, translate the problem and execute, and Theorem 4 localises every remaining uncertainty of the language model to the translation step, with the execution share of errors provably zero. Second, the certificate is a measurement of a deployed artifact in its real numerics, which tables do not need and neural deployments do (quantisation flipped margins from +9.4 to −8.9). Then concede the limit plainly: execution is certified; perception and translation are not. Enough for NC. Enough for NMI only if the localisation is shown to buy something, which is the application upgrade.

**Joint ranking (three items).**
1. **The application where the guarantee matters** (your 2, my 1): the 4B chat model with the sheet on long-number word problems, against the base model, a Python-tool baseline and frontier models; report the arithmetic share of errors as provably zero and the translation share measured. Required for either venue.
2. **Method, not model:** certify the same tables on two or three other bases (my 3), cite DNAR and Lewandowski with the distinctions stated, and add a CLRS subset only if the DNAR comparison is explicit and the pretrained-plus-device increment is the stated claim.
3. **Robustness:** device generality across GPUs (your 3) and the forced-answer Theorem 2 panel (my 2, demoted).

**Revised single recommendation.** The headline is the application, not the theory: "a general model, its other abilities intact, carries a certificate for unbounded computation on its own hardware, and that certificate localises its remaining errors to translation and measurably improves it." Submit to NC after items 1 and 2 (four to five weeks, under $800). Go to NMI first only if item 1 yields a large, clean gain over tool-free baselines; your NMI-then-transfer sequence costs two to four weeks on a desk rejection and nothing else, so it is a fair choice if the author is not time-constrained. Post to arXiv now, with the Lewandowski and DNAR distinctions in the text.

### Correction on Lewandowski, after Opus's check

**Withdrawn.** I claimed their certificate "rests on an assumption no finite test can verify". It does not. Their decoding loop keeps the operational string outside the model; each step's input is the system prompt plus the encoded symbol pair ("appended to the system prompt and given to the language model as input"), Theorem 1 is stated for M(S E(s1) E(s2)), and the extension to all inputs is an induction over verified rule outputs. That is a pinned policy, the same as the sheet. The paper's own Introduction frames them correctly as sharing the observation; the error was mine. Theorem 2 is therefore a contrast with chain of thought, not with them.

**Honest distinction, ranked by weight.** (a) The certificate: margins in the deployment numerics with a robustness theorem, bound to open weights by hash and recomputable on the user's device. Theirs is exact match at temperature zero on a server with no floating-point account, and their Gemini-1.5 proof is already unreproducible because the model was deprecated; a certificate that survives deployment is a real and sayable difference. (c) A 0.8B model trained to meet the condition, against a prompted 17B. (d) Procedures with measured performance, composed into algebra. (e) The exact gate. (b) and (f) are supporting: Theorem 2 is Moore, and Lean adds rigour, not reach.

**Re-assessment.** This worsens novelty at both venues, modestly. The paper's one conceptual distinction becomes quantitative and engineering; an editor can now say "Lewandowski certified a universal computer, this certifies a calculator, more carefully." NMI: sent to review ~20%, overall ~5–6%. NC: overall ~17–18%. Upgrade 4 (a general interpreter) is dead: it would be "Lewandowski with a small trained model and device margins", narrower on the axis they own. A CLRS subset is dead for the same reason via DNAR. What gains weight is everything that deepens (a): device generality across GPUs, cross-model certification, and the reproducibility argument made explicit (a hash-bound certificate anyone can recompute, against proofs that die with a model version).

**Revised joint ranking.** 1. The application where the guarantee matters (unchanged; neither Lewandowski nor DNAR shows a use). 2. The certificate as the method: device generality, cross-model, reproducibility, with Lewandowski and DNAR as named comparators. 3. The forced-answer Theorem 2 panel, supporting only. **Recommendation** unchanged in venue (NC after 1 and 2; NMI only if 1 is striking), changed in framing: the method claim is "a certificate that survives deployment", and the headline is the application.
