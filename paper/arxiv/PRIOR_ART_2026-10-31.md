# Prior-art check before posting, 31 October 2026

Paper: "Sketchpad: certified exact arithmetic with a small vision-language model" (`paper/paper.md`).
Previous check: `the research archive's research/audit/sketchpad3_gate/out.md` (30 Oct; verdict NARROW). This check looks for work posted since then and for anything that check missed. I did not repeat the papers it covered.

## Verdict

**The novelty sentence stands. No sentence of `paper.md` needs to change because of a new competitor, and five related-work sentences should gain citations.** In ten days of arXiv listings (17–31 Oct) and in a wider 2025–2026 sweep, I found nothing that combines what the paper combines: a pretrained model, a finite set of views that holds by construction, an exhaustive certificate over that set, and a re-check on the deployment device. An arXiv search for "exact arithmetic" together with any vision-language term returned **zero** papers. I read the abstracts of 43 papers. None pre-empts the paper, but four are close to individual parts of it:

- **The gate (contribution 5):** modality-gated deep adapters (Tonmoy et al., Aug 2026) and Activated LoRA (Greenewald et al., 2025) already offer a guarantee of the same kind: the base model's outputs are exactly unchanged where the adapter is off.
- **The certificate (contribution 2):** compact proofs (Gross et al., NeurIPS 2024) prove a trained transformer's accuracy by enumerating its inputs. MDL networks (Lan et al., TACL 2022) come with proofs that addition is exact on inputs of any length.

The paper should cite all four. It should also cite three older ancestors that the 29 Oct "penpaper" check found but `paper.md` still omits:
- Neural Programmer-Interpreters, a scratch pad read through pointers, one step at a time;
- Zaremba et al. 2016, a controller on a 2-D grid that adds numbers of thousands of digits;
- Hoshen and Peleg 2016, arithmetic learned from pictures of numbers.

One contrast also needs rewording: "certifying exactness by enumeration rather than estimating it" is inaccurate once papers that *prove* exactness are cited beside it. Proposed text is below.

## Terms used here (one line each)

- **Prior art:** earlier published work that already does what a paper claims; a "pre-empting" paper would make a novelty sentence false.
- **Abstract read vs. snippet-only:** "abstract read" means I fetched the paper's own abstract from arXiv. "Snippet-only" means I saw only a search engine's summary, which can be wrong.
- **Activated LoRA (aLoRA):** a LoRA adapter that is applied only to tokens after the point where it is invoked. The base model's stored attention keys and values (its *KV cache*) for earlier tokens can therefore be reused unchanged. It matters here because it is the closest existing "switch the adapter on in the middle of a reply" mechanism.
- **Modality-gated adapter:** small extra layers (*bottleneck adapters*) that run only while inputs of one modality, such as audio, are being encoded. Every other input passes through the frozen base model bit for bit unchanged. It matters because it makes the same exact-preservation claim as our gate.
- **MDL network:** a small network trained to minimise its *minimum description length*, the size of the network plus the cost of its errors. The resulting networks are simple enough to prove correct by hand.
- **Compact proof / brute-force proof:** a computer-checked proof that a trained network is accurate. The brute-force version simply runs every possible input. It matters because it is verification by enumeration of a trained transformer, though on a fixed-size input.
- **Neural Programmer-Interpreter (NPI):** a 2016 recurrent network that learns programs such as grade-school addition. It acts one step at a time on a scratch pad read through *pointers* (movable read positions). Cai et al. 2017, which the paper cites, builds directly on it.
- **Bit-identical:** equal in every bit of the floating-point numbers, not merely close.

## Proposed sentence changes

None of these changes is forced by a paper that pre-empts the work. All of them add missing citations or fix a contrast. **The novelty sentence, the contribution list and the abstract can stay as they are.** For the novelty sentence ("Our contribution is a system in which the hypothesis holds by construction for a pretrained model, together with a certificate measured on the deployment device."), no hit has a pretrained model, a finite view set that holds by construction, or a certificate measured on the device.

**C1. Neural arithmetic (Related work).** Original:

> "Mechanistic analyses find addition circuits correct on almost all inputs [@quirke2024verified], and exactness has been proved under idealised training [@backdeluca2025exact]. Sketchpad differs in using a pretrained vision-language model, in covering division, square roots and gcd, and in certifying exactness by enumeration rather than estimating it."

Proposed:

> "Mechanistic analyses find addition circuits correct on almost all inputs [@quirke2024verified]; exactness has been proved under idealised training [@backdeluca2025exact], for addition by small networks trained with a description-length objective, on inputs of any length [@lan2022mdl], and for a transformer parameterisation that learns Boolean circuits and modular arithmetic [@ito2026circuits]. Sketchpad differs in using a pretrained vision-language model, in covering division, square roots and gcd, and in certifying the trained weights by enumeration, where these works estimate accuracy on samples or prove exactness for idealised, hand-analysed or purpose-built networks."

Reason: Lan et al. 2022 already proves exact, length-general addition for a learned network, and Ito et al. 2026 proves exact length generalisation for a learned transformer. Beside them, "rather than estimating it" understates the prior work. A reviewer who knows Lan et al. would flag the original sentence.

**C2. Procedures in language models (Related work).** Original:

> "CAEF fine-tunes Llama-3.1-8B to execute arithmetic operators step by step on a text tape, with one adapter per operator [@lai2024caef]."

Proposed:

> "Neural programmer-interpreters learned grade-school addition as a sequence of steps on a scratch pad read through pointers [@reed2016npi], the setting whose generalisation Cai et al. verified [@cai2017recursion], and a controller moving over a two-dimensional grid learned multi-digit addition that extends to thousands of digits [@zaremba2016simple]. CAEF fine-tunes Llama-3.1-8B to execute arithmetic operators step by step on a text tape, with one adapter per operator [@lai2024caef]."

Reason: NPI is the direct ancestor of the sheet machine, with one step per call, a scratch pad and local reads. The paper cites Cai et al. 2017, which builds on NPI, but not NPI itself. Both NPI and Zaremba et al. were found in the 29 Oct check and never cited.

**C3. Verification (Related work).** Original:

> "Neural network verification typically bounds outputs over continuous input regions [@katz2017reluplex]; here the input set is finite."

Proposed:

> "Neural network verification typically bounds outputs over continuous input regions [@katz2017reluplex]; here the input set is finite, as in brute-force proofs of a small transformer's accuracy on a fixed-size task [@gross2024compact], except that a finite set of views covers inputs of every length."

Reason: Gross et al. (NeurIPS 2024) is a published instance of verifying a trained transformer by enumerating its inputs. Citing it heads off a "this is just brute force" review and states the actual difference: the finite set covers unbounded lengths. Optionally add Somani 2026 ([2605.24033](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24033)) after Gross et al., for solver checks over a fixed prompt domain.

**C4. Visual input and scratchpads (Related work).** Original:

> "Pixel-based language models [@rust2023pixel; @tschannen2023clippo; @lee2023pix2struct] and visual context compression [@wei2025deepseekocr; @cheng2025glyph] read rendered text, though vision-language models still fail at multiplication from images [@balter2026vlm]."

Proposed:

> "Pixel-based language models [@rust2023pixel; @tschannen2023clippo; @lee2023pix2struct] and visual context compression [@wei2025deepseekocr; @cheng2025glyph] read rendered text. A small network learned seven-digit addition, but not multiplication, from pictures of numbers [@hoshen2016visual], and vision-language models still fail at multiplication from images [@balter2026vlm]."

Reason: Hoshen and Peleg 2016 is the earliest arithmetic on rendered digits; it was found on 29 Oct and not cited.
