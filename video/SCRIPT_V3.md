## Read and timing

Target: **about 4:28**, including breaths, transitions and the final links. Times below are
an editorial budget, not measured audio durations. Stage 2 must time the Cedar reading before
locking the picture. Budget roughly 165–170 spoken words per minute; the montage and close
carry the remaining picture time. Voice: `gpt-audio-1.5`, Cedar; warm, curious, conversational. Read the
questions as questions someone watching the previous picture would actually ask. Do not race
through the qualifications or speak the source keys and production notes.

Each numbered narration row is one sentence. Leave **0.8 seconds after every sentence**,
except at the chapter boundaries marked **2.0 seconds**; the longer pause replaces the
shorter one. The montage has additional silent picture time. A new subject must be visible
at least **0.6 seconds before** its first spoken name. Within a sentence, let commas breathe
naturally; those are not additional timed gaps. The chapter pauses carry a change in the
music and continued visual action, never a blank screen.

At 0:23 the multiplication is already running; division starts at 0:28, algebra at 0:33,
and chat at 0:39. Thus all four kinds of work appear by the first forty seconds. These are
moving captures of Recorded mode, not screenshots. The brief title occupies 0:21–0:23.

### Capture and movement contract for Stage 2

- Capture the live demo page in **Recorded mode**, at 60 frames per second, with no model
  loaded. This mode plays recorded model outputs through the working interface; it is not
  new inference. Keep “Recorded” visible, or add the same short caption when the UI label
  falls outside the framing. The four JSON files name the performances to replay; they are
  not substitutes for capturing the interface in motion.
- Capture enough time before, during and after each run for the transitions above. Preserve
  complete sheet bounds, operands, working area and result. Reduce surrounding page chrome
  before shrinking the sheet; expand the capture height for algebra and chat. Never crop off
  an intermediate line or result to make the frame look cleaner.
- The early montage uses a complete moving multiplication and division, an accelerated
  cubic derivation if needed, and the chat sheet opening and returning its result. Preserve
  event order under acceleration and label it; do not imply the edit measures inference speed.
- Reuse `lib.js`, `win.js`, `grid.js`, the real glyph sprites and v1's white/Cobalt/Rose look.
  Source Serif titles; Geist interface captions; Geist Mono digits. Put one short caption
  or key number on screen at a time, apart from necessary demo UI and the final links.
- Every new line has a defined source and target. Reveal its stroke along that direction,
  then its arrowhead, then its label. Apply this to the idea, proof, margin, training and
  gate scenes and to chart axes. No centre-grown lines, instantaneous replacement objects,
  or decorative drifting screenshots. Keep a visual anchor alive through each transition.
- Music follows the story: opening uncertainty; a warmer motif with the working sheet;
  measured ticks for the local steps; more space at the proof and rounding catch; a brief
  lift at the comparison; restraint at the chat cost; resolution at the invitation.
